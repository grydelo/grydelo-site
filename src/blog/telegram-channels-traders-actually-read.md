## Choose channel or group before you post

A Telegram channel broadcasts. Only admins post. Followers read, react, and sometimes reply in threaded comments. A group lets everyone talk. Both can work for a broker, but they solve different problems.

Use a channel for:

- Scheduled market notes, session opens, and education series.
- One-way distribution where the message must stay clean.
- Regions where traders expect a broadcast feed, not a chat room.

Use a group for:

- Community discussion around live events or webinars.
- Support triage when you have moderators on shift.
- Feedback loops with a small, engaged segment.

Many brokers run both. Keep the channel as the primary feed. Link to the group from pinned posts, and state the rules in the group description. If you cannot moderate a group daily, do not open one. An unmoderated group becomes a spam folder within weeks.

## Pace posts to the trading session

Traders read at different times. A single daily digest rarely fits everyone. Map your cadence to the sessions your audience trades.

### Asia session

- A short pre-open note with the calendar and overnight moves.
- One chart or level summary. No predictions, no signals.
- Keep it under 200 words. Mobile readers skim.

### London open

- A session open post 15 to 30 minutes before the cash open.
- What changed since Asia. Key data releases due that morning.
- A link to the day's education piece if one is scheduled.

### New York overlap

- A mid-session update only if something material happened.
- A closing note with a recap and a question to prompt comments.

Three to five posts a day is a workable rhythm for most brokers. More than that and mute rates climb. Fewer and the channel feels dormant. Test one cadence for a month, then adjust based on reads, not on how busy the team feels.

## Formats that earn a read

### Morning notes

A fixed template trains readers. Same time, same structure, same length. Include the date, the main themes, and a compliance-approved disclaimer. Avoid forecasts. Describe what happened and what is scheduled.

### Session open posts

Short. One idea per post. A chart image with clear labels works better than a wall of text. If you cannot explain the chart in two sentences, cut it.

### Education series

Number the episodes. A ten-part series on risk management, platform tools, or order types gives readers a reason to return. Publish on the same weekday. Keep each episode to a single concept. Link back to earlier episodes in a pinned index.

### Polls and questions

Use polls to learn what your audience trades and when. Use open questions to surface topics for the education series. Both give you content ideas without guessing.

## Moderate without killing the room

Set rules before you need them. Publish them in the channel description and pin them in the group.

- No signals, no account recommendations, no profit claims.
- No links to third-party groups or copy-trading schemes.
- No personal data in public messages. Redirect to official support.
- No harassment. One warning, then removal.

Staff the group with at least two moderators across your main trading hours. Use Telegram's admin tools: slow mode, restricted media, and keyword filters. Log every removal with a reason. If a topic keeps causing trouble, ban the topic, not just the user.

Train moderators to answer compliance questions with a standard line: check with your compliance team before making any claim about products, bonuses, or regulation. Never let a moderator improvise on regulated topics.

## Handle impersonators before they spread

Impersonators copy your name, logo, and tone. They message your followers with fake account managers or fake bonus offers. Prepare a response now.

- Claim your brand name and close variants on Telegram.
- Publish the only official channel and group links in every bio and on your website.
- Pin a post explaining how to verify an admin. Real admins never ask for deposits via direct message.
- Ask followers to report impersonators to Telegram and to your support address.
- Keep a short, pre-approved warning post ready to publish within an hour of a reported clone.

Do not argue with impersonators in public. Report, warn, and move on. Every hour a fake channel stays live costs trust.

## Measure retention, not subscriber counts

Subscriber numbers flatter. Retention tells you whether the channel works.

Track:

- Read rate per post, where Telegram shows it.
- Mute and leave rates over a rolling 30-day window.
- Replies and reactions per post, split by format.
- Clicks to your website or education hub, tagged by post type.
- Cohort retention: of the people who joined in a given month, how many still read after 30 and 90 days.

A channel that adds 5,000 subscribers and loses 4,000 in a quarter is not growing. A channel that adds 500 and keeps 400 has a base you can build on.

Review the numbers monthly. Cut formats that underperform. Double the cadence of the ones that hold attention. Ask new joiners one question at onboarding: which session do you trade? Use the answers to shape your posting times.

## Checklist

- Decide channel or group, and state the purpose in the description.
- Publish rules and a pinned index of education episodes.
- Set a cadence by session: Asia, London, New York overlap.
- Use fixed templates for morning notes and session opens.
- Staff at least two moderators and log every removal.
- Claim brand variants and keep an impersonator warning post ready.
- Review read rate, mute rate, and 30/90-day cohort retention monthly.
